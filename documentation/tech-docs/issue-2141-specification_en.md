## 2. Context and objectives

When tracking of underlying device changes is enabled, a TRV must be able to change the VTherm setpoint without its own command echoes causing resends, priority reversals, or Home Assistant overload. The current code discards events arriving less than 10 seconds after a VTherm change even for a distinct action; after that interval, an old return may be treated as a new action. A comparison using the VTherm step also discards some returns before they can be examined.

The functional scope is bidirectional tracking **of setpoints** for an `over_climate` with one or more TRVs when the existing tracking option is enabled. This specification defines target behavior, not an already delivered feature. Preventing loops that would overload HA takes precedence over capturing every manual action: without reliable provenance, some ambiguous actions can be lost and no zero-loss guarantee may be claimed.

Only one target interval applies: a sliding five-second wait from the **last new TRV candidate value** before possible adoption. For each TRV, only its last actually sent command is used to reject as an action a report that is equal, compatible with verified quantization, or too close to distinguish conservatively; superseded commands are not kept for classification, and no echo horizon or per-TRV latency interval is required. Neither proximity nor expiry of the five seconds proves provenance. An older echo distinct from the latest command may be adopted by mistake, notably after a `7 -> 21` sequence or preset restoration. One cap of **3 tracking-induced physical command attempts per VTherm** bounds attempts across all destination TRVs without interrupting direct control; it does not alone guarantee the absence of internal updates without a command, which require coalescing no-op actions.

Every new command received by VTherm at its external entry point (UI, automation, or HA service) resets this cap to zero and rearms tracking, even if the automation reacts to the TRV: no proof of independence of the external sender's original cause is required. Only physical attempts caused by internal tracking of a device/TRV report debit the cap; their deferred consequences and the regulation they induce retain that cause and do not rearm. A reactive external automation can therefore rearm on every turn: the cap of 3 bounds attempts **between new received commands**, not an entire external loop.

Three values must remain separate: the **VTherm requested setpoint** (displayed intent), the **setpoint actually sent** to each TRV (possibly regulated, compensated, rounded, or clamped), and the **setpoint observed** in that TRV's physical report. A successful service call or a stored sent value does not prove that the TRV acknowledged the order.

## 3. Actors and use cases

- **User:** sets a VTherm setpoint or turns a TRV knob; consults tracking status and diagnostics.
- **`over_climate` VTherm:** retains its requested setpoint and arbitrates TRV observations against newer intentions and higher-priority functions.
- **Underlying TRV:** receives a setpoint and reports its physical value, sometimes quantized or delayed; it does not necessarily provide change provenance.
- **Home Assistant / maintainer:** exposes states and service calls, observes load, and validates scenarios before development and then controlled deployment.

**Nominal case:** tracking is enabled; a TRV reports `26.0` after `25.6` was sent, distinct from the last command sent to that TRV and not too close to distinguish; VTherm retains `26.0` without immediately adopting it. If the last candidate value is still present for five seconds after its last change and no higher-priority intention intervenes, VTherm adopts it once in `requested_state`; it switches to a manual setpoint without a preset and controls the other TRVs without their reports bouncing back. If the effective mode is OFF, the admissible action updates the request without removing the forced OFF or turning on a manually OFF VTherm.

**Ambiguous case:** after sending `25.6`, a TRV with step `0.5` reports `25.5`, compatible with its quantization. VTherm keeps `25.6`, records `25.5` as an observation, and sends no correction. A human action to `25.5` at the same time is indistinguishable without provenance and may not be tracked.

## 4. Functional requirements

- **FR-001 — Scope:** The system shall track setpoints only for `over_climate` VTherms with underlying-change tracking enabled; when tracking is disabled, it shall preserve VTherm control without adopting a TRV setpoint.
- **FR-002 — Distinct values:** The system shall distinguish the requested VTherm setpoint, the value actually sent to each TRV, and the value observed for that TRV, including under regulation, compensation, clamping, and differing steps.
- **FR-003 — Report qualification:** The system shall separately qualify for each TRV reports equal to or compatible with only its last actually sent command, distinct reports, and observations too close to distinguish. It shall keep no history of superseded commands for this classification; numerical proximity or expiry of the wait shall prove neither human action nor a certain echo.
- **FR-004 — Quantized echo:** The system shall retain the requested VTherm setpoint when a TRV reports a value compatible with the verified transformation of its last sent setpoint; it shall keep the physical observation without changing the preset or resending a corrective command for that difference alone, without claiming with certainty that the report is an echo.
- **FR-005 — Distinct change:** While tracking is admissible and a valid TRV value differs from the last sent command and unclassifiable nearby values, the system shall retain it as a candidate without a rejection period tied to the VTherm order. It shall adopt it only after five seconds without a new candidate value, if that last value is still present, no newer higher-priority intention exists, and the other guards pass; it shall update the request exactly once when admissible, including while OFF for any cause, without admitting an ambiguous action.
- **FR-006 — Ambiguity:** Without reliable provenance, the system shall treat a report compatible with an echo, or close under unverified device capabilities, as ambiguous: it shall neither adopt it automatically nor promise to track every manual action.
- **FR-007 — Superseded orders and classification limit:** The system shall compare a report only with the last command actually sent to its TRV, even when it arrives late or out of order. It shall not claim to recognize an echo of a superseded command: if that report is distinct and admissible, it may be adopted by mistake after five seconds of stability, with no general rejection guarantee. Physical commands caused by such adoption shall remain subject to the FR-010 cap and BR-008 suspension; neither echoes nor timers shall rearm that cap.
- **FR-008 — Rapid actions:** The system shall wait a sliding five seconds from the last **new candidate TRV setpoint value** for each TRV: each distinct new value replaces the previous one and restarts the wait. During a burst it shall neither adopt nor recalculate the VTherm setpoint nor issue an order caused by this tracking. A subsequent echo or ambiguous observation that physically replaces the candidate shall cancel or requalify it; a value that is no longer current shall never be applied. An identical duplicate or an event without a setpoint change shall not extend the wait.
- **FR-009 — Multiple TRVs:** The system shall arbitrate observations and orders independently for each TRV under the shared FR-010 cap, without a slow TRV automatically blocking others; it shall avoid an unnecessary resend to the source TRV already at its effective physical value and avoid rebounds from other TRVs.
- **FR-010 — Loop prevention:** The system shall apply one cap of **3 physical command attempts caused by internal tracking of a device/TRV report per VTherm**, across all destination TRVs, including retries, deferred sends, induced regulation, and uncertain results. It shall debit each attempt before the physical call: admit and debit the third, then suspend tracking and reject the fourth and subsequent attempts before any call. It shall preserve the internal cause across deferred callbacks and induced regulation without allowing those paths to rearm tracking. It shall coalesce no-op actions and avoid redundant resends to limit internal updates without commands as well, without claiming that this cap alone bounds them. Suspension shall not block direct VTherm-to-TRV control; no adoption counter or per-TRV cap is required.
- **FR-011 — Lifecycle:** The system shall cancel candidates and their waits on a new VTherm intention, tracking deactivation, TRV loss/unavailability, or restart, without orphaned deferred adoption; a restored state alone shall not count as a manual action. Entering OFF shall cancel an earlier candidate but shall not preclude a later admissible action while OFF. After restart or loss of the last sent command, tracking shall remain suspended if the counter is lost until a new command is received by VTherm at its external entry point (UI, automation, or HA service), even if its sender reacts to the TRV; neither restart nor restoration shall rearm it. Resumption remains subject to FR-012 and BR-008, without requiring impossible proof of maximum latency.
- **FR-012 — First order:** Until a setpoint has actually been sent to the TRV since initialization, the system shall not automatically adopt its setpoint as a manual action; any extension of this rule requires an explicit decision.
- **FR-013 — Non-regression:** The system shall not implicitly change handling of HVAC modes, `hvac_action`, or fan modes when processing TRV setpoints alone; their relevant updates, as well as sensor temperature and attributes without a setpoint change, shall continue independently without extending the stabilization wait.
- **FR-014 — Diagnostics and documentation:** The system shall make requested setpoint, last sent command per TRV, and observed value, possible echo, pending or rejected action, cancellation, debited attempts, saturation, and recovery status distinguishable in usable diagnostics; user documentation shall explain the sole sliding five-second wait, ambiguous reports, the risk of adopting an old echo and an erroneous manual preset, the cap's limits for internal updates, suspension, rearming by any new command received at VTherm's external entry point, and how to disable tracking. It shall state that an HA automation reacting to the TRV can rearm on every turn, so the cap of 3 applies only between received commands, with no global guarantee against this external loop.

## 5. Business rules

- **BR-001 — Safety priority:** Preventing a loop that overloads HA shall take precedence over adopting an ambiguous TRV action; an expired wait is neither provenance nor acknowledgement.
- **BR-002 — Latest intention:** A preset, window closing/opening, VTherm shutdown, override restoration, or other newer VTherm intention shall invalidate an earlier candidate; a later admissible TRV action, including while OFF for any cause, shall update `requested_state.target_temperature` and switch to a manual setpoint (`requested_state.preset = NONE`). It shall cancel any old timed override without subsequent restoration and shall not start a new one. It shall neither remove a forced effective OFF while its cause persists nor change `requested_state.hvac_mode` when it is OFF; existing temperature priorities, notably window and protection, remain unchanged.
- **BR-003 — Compatible echo:** For a TRV whose step and quantization behavior have been verified, `25.6` requested and sent followed by `25.5` observed with step `0.5` shall be treated as a possible echo, without switching VTherm to `25.5`, changing its preset, or sending another `25.6` correction.
- **BR-004 — Indeterminate origin:** A human action to `25.5` concurrent with this echo shall follow the same conservative policy; only usable provenance or a later distinct observation can disambiguate it.
- **BR-005 — Superseded sequence:** After sending `7` during a window event and then `21` when VTherm priority resumes, only `21` is the last known command for that TRV. A late `7` report distinct from `21` may be admitted after the wait if the other guards pass, mistakenly causing a setpoint or manual preset; a `21` report equal or compatible shall be rejected as an ambiguous action. The risk of adopting `7` is accepted; induced physical attempts remain subject to BR-008.
- **BR-006 — Unknown capability:** If the necessary rounding rule, TRV step, or provenance is missing, the system shall reject nearby unclassifiable values as actions; it shall assume neither that the TRV step is the VTherm step, nor universal tolerance, proof of origin from the step, or implicit acknowledgement.
- **BR-007 — Admissible transition:** A distinct, valid TRV value may be adopted only after five seconds since its last change, if the latest Home Assistant observation confirms it remains current, tracking is enabled and not suspended, a prior order has been issued since initialization, and no report compatible with the last command, ambiguity, or newer intention has replaced it. Each distinct new value restarts the wait; a compatible or ambiguous report replacing the physical value cancels or requalifies the candidate, never allowing the former value to be adopted. Even when VTherm is OFF, adoption updates the requested temperature and switches to a manual setpoint under BR-002; if OFF is forced, the request takes effect under normal calculation when its cause clears, and if OFF is manual, only after explicit switch-on. No window or protection priority is changed; no ambiguous action passes this gate.
- **BR-008 — Bounds and recovery:** The only interval is BR-007's sliding five seconds after the last new TRV candidate value; its expiry alone shall not make a compatible report admissible. The system shall apply one cap of **3 physical command attempts caused by internal tracking of a device/TRV report per VTherm**, across all its TRVs, including retries, deferred sends, induced regulation, and uncertain results. Each attempt shall be debited before the call: the third shall be admitted and debited, then tracking suspended with an observable reason; the fourth and subsequent attempts shall be rejected before any call, without blocking direct control. Every new command received by VTherm at its external entry point (UI, automation, or HA service) shall reset the counter to zero and rearm tracking, including after restart with a lost counter and even when the external sender reacts to the TRV; no proof of independence of its original cause shall be required. An echo, timer, autonomous regulation, internal callback, tracking-derived command, tracking-option toggle, passage of time, or restart alone shall not count as a new received command and shall not rearm tracking. Deferred callbacks and regulation induced by tracking shall retain that internal cause without rearming, even when they use the entry point of an external command.
- **BR-009 — No acknowledgement:** A silent TRV shall neither trigger automatic adoption of a report nor indefinitely block control from VTherm; continuation of tracking remains limited by BR-008, with a diagnostic on suspension. A report equal to a sent value or a successful service call does not prove older echoes are gone.

## 6. Functional constraints

- **Availability / safety:** Physical attempts caused by internal tracking shall remain capped at 3 per VTherm between new received commands, even with repeated reports, different steps, slow TRVs, or missing reports; that cap alone does not guarantee the absence of internal updates without commands, hence coalescing no-op actions. An external HA automation reacting to the TRV and commanding VTherm can rearm on every turn: the cap does not provide a global bound against that external loop. Validation in a test environment and a way to disable tracking must precede experimentation on a sensitive installation.
- **Compatibility:** Preserve the existing switch and VTherm control when tracking is disabled. Do not implicitly alter HVAC modes, fan modes, regulation, TRV integrations, or global safety-function priorities.
- **Privacy:** No new personal data or external service is required; diagnostics shall be proportionate and avoid excessive noise.
- **Precision / experience:** The displayed VTherm setpoint shall not be replaced by a quantized physical value. A TRV without usable provenance can guarantee neither zero lost actions nor rejection of a distinct old echo that may incorrectly trigger a manual preset; separately presenting the physical value to users remains undecided (Q-04).
- **Parameters and recovery:** The sole sliding wait from the last new TRV candidate setpoint is five seconds, without an echo horizon or per-TRV latency profile; the cap is 3 attempts per VTherm between new commands received at the external entry point and each such command rearms it without proof of causal independence. Continuing actions less than five seconds apart defer adoption until they stop; users shall be able to understand this wait. Recovery eliminates neither the risk of older echoes nor that of a loop sustained by a reactive external automation.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

The criteria concern the VTherm setpoint, preset/override, observed values, service calls, and diagnostics; they specify observable results without imposing an internal algorithm.

- **AC-001:** Tracking disabled: changing the TRV does not change the VTherm setpoint, and VTherm can still control the TRV.
- **AC-002:** `25.6` requested and sent to a step-`0.5` TRV whose quantization to `25.5` is verified: on a `25.5` report, VTherm remains at `25.6`, preset unchanged, `25.5` available for diagnostics, and no new corrective order caused by that report.
- **AC-003:** In the same context, with cap available, `26.0` distinct from the last command sent to the TRV and unclassifiable nearby values may become a candidate without waiting after the VTherm order; it does not change the request or issue a tracking-induced order until five seconds without a new candidate value. If `26.0` is still the latest HA value, with no compatible command or new priority, it then updates the request exactly once; if `26.5` and then `27.0` each arrive within five seconds, no adoption or tracking-induced order occurs before five seconds after `27.0`, as specified in AC-008. The adopted action removes the preset and cancels any old timed override without restoration, under BR-002.
- **AC-004:** An action to `25.5` indistinguishable from the echo does not overwrite `25.6` or cause a corrective order; diagnostics mark it ambiguous and documentation does not promise to capture it.
- **AC-005:** With unknown step or rounding, a nearby report alone cannot change VTherm; no feedback loop results.
- **AC-006:** A superseded command returns late, including out of order: it is not recognized from history. If it differs from the last command sent to that TRV and remains admissible after five seconds, mistaken adoption, possibly with a manual preset, is a documented risk. Every physical attempt caused by this tracking, including a retry, deferred send to another TRV, or uncertain result, is debited before the call from the VTherm's single cap of 3; the third is admitted, then tracking suspended with an observable reason and the fourth rejected before any call. Direct control remains possible. No-op actions are coalesced, without claiming that the cap alone bounds all internal updates.
- **AC-007:** VTherm sends `7`, then `21` after a window closes; the TRV reports `7` late while `21` is the last sent command. `7` is not recognized as an old echo: if it remains the last admissible candidate value after five seconds, it may be adopted by mistake and switch to preset `NONE`, without changing existing priorities; physical attempts caused by this adoption remain capped under AC-006. A `21` report equal or compatible with the last command is rejected as an ambiguous action. If `21` physically replaces `7` before the deadline, `7` is not adopted.
- **AC-008:** `26.0 -> 26.5 -> 27.0` on one TRV, each new value arriving less than five seconds after the previous one: the candidate is replaced and the five seconds restart on each value; there is no adoption, VTherm setpoint recalculation, or tracking-induced order during the burst. If `27.0` is still the HA value and admissible after five seconds with no new value, exactly one adoption of `27.0` occurs. If real actions continue less than five seconds apart, no adoption occurs until they stop. An identical `27.0` duplicate or a sensor-temperature, `hvac_action`, HVAC-mode, fan-mode, or other attribute change without a setpoint change neither advances nor restarts the wait and continues to be handled independently. A known echo or ambiguous value that physically replaces `27.0` cancels or requalifies the candidate: `27.0` cannot then be adopted, even when its old wait expires; a stable echo does not become admissible solely because time elapsed. A new VTherm intention also cancels the candidate.
- **AC-009:** Two TRVs with different steps and latencies: a slow echo from one does not block a distinct action on the other while the shared VTherm cap is available; the source TRV already at its effective value receives no unnecessary resend and the others do not feed their compatible reports back as actions.
- **AC-010:** With regulation/compensation/clamping, the sent value can differ from the request; compatible reports do not replace the request, and a distinct value is evaluated only against the last command actually sent to its own TRV.
- **AC-011:** Override restoration, a preset, window priority, or shutdown during the five-second wait cancels the candidate and prevents adoption at the old deadline; in particular, entering OFF after `26.0` became a candidate does not validate `26.0` when the wait ends. A **later admissible TRV** action during forced OFF, whatever its cause, waits its own five seconds then updates `requested_state.target_temperature` and sets `requested_state.preset = NONE` without removing effective OFF. Under manual OFF it preserves `requested_state.hvac_mode = OFF` and waits for explicit switch-on. An ambiguous value never updates the request. If an old timed override would expire **before** OFF ends, the admissible TRV action cancels that override without restoration or a new timed override: the saved request remains applicable when the cause clears or after explicit switch-on, under normal calculation and subject to existing temperature priorities. Also check the saved request, preset `NONE`, and continued effective priorities under a formerly active preset, window, or protection; none of those priorities is changed by the action.
- **AC-012:** Tracking deactivation, TRV removal/unavailability, or restart during the sliding five-second wait: no candidate or restored state is applied later; cancelled waits cause no orphaned transition at their old deadline. After restart with a lost counter, tracking remains suspended even if a first order is sent by autonomous regulation; neither restart alone nor toggling the tracking option rearms it. A new command received by VTherm from the UI, an automation, or an HA service resets the counter to zero and rearms tracking even if the automation reacts to the TRV; after a fresh first order actually issued to the TRV, a new distinct action is admissible only if tracking is enabled and five seconds of stability have elapsed. An old echo may be misclassified; direct control remains possible even if tracking is suspended.
- **AC-013:** Without TRV acknowledgement, under repeated or oscillating echoes and with multiple TRVs, three physical attempts caused by internal tracking (including retries, deferred sends, induced regulation, and uncertain results) are debited before their calls across all destinations; the third is admitted, then tracking suspended with an observable reason. A fourth and subsequent attempts are rejected before any call; no echo, timer, autonomous regulation, internal callback, tracking-derived order, option toggle, or restart alone resets the counter. Each new command received by VTherm from the UI, an automation, or an HA service resets the counter to zero and permits up to three attempts again, even when the automation reacts to the TRV, without requiring proof of independence of its original cause; direct control remains possible. A deferred callback or regulation induced by a TRV report does not rearm, even when using the entry point of an external command. If an HA automation reacts to each TRV report with a new command to VTherm, it can rearm on every turn: the cap of 3 applies between received commands, not to the entire external chain. No-op actions are coalesced to avoid a cascade of internal updates without commands, without claiming that the cap alone forbids all of them.
- **AC-014:** Before the first actually sent order, a TRV or restored value is not automatically adopted; successful sending does not count as acknowledgement.
- **AC-015:** An event containing a pending or echo-classified setpoint observation along with a sensor-temperature, `hvac_action`, HVAC-mode, fan-mode, or other attribute update does not suppress independent handling of those fields; without a new candidate setpoint value it does not extend the sliding wait, and HVAC mode receives no new implicit rule.
- **AC-016:** Documentation and diagnostics explain the three values, the sole sliding five-second debounce from the last new TRV candidate value, comparison with the last sent command per TRV, the wait extended by successive actions, ambiguity, the risk of adopting an old echo and an erroneous manual preset, the single cap of 3 physical attempts and its limits for internal updates, refusal before a call after the third, rejection and suspension, rearming by any new command received at VTherm's external entry point, and how to disable tracking. They disclose possible rearming on every turn by an external automation reacting to the TRV and the lack of a global bound against that loop, without a zero-loss or perfect-classification guarantee.

## 8. Out-of-scope functions

- Modifying TRV integrations or adding device provenance: such information has not been established as available.
- Reworking regulation, HVAC modes, fan modes, and global safety priorities: outside the setpoint tracking request.
- Guaranteeing that **all** actions indistinguishable from echoes are tracked: impossible from setpoint observation alone.
- Guaranteeing rejection of **all** arbitrarily late echoes: unavailable without reliable provenance; functional risk accepted with a cap on tracking-induced physical commands.
- Guaranteeing a global bound of 3 attempts against an external HA automation that reacts to the TRV by sending new VTherm commands: each received command rearms, even if its sender reacts to the TRV; only the bound between received commands is guaranteed.
- Automatic adoption before the first VTherm order: deferred pending an approved initialization policy (Q-03).
- Migrating HA configuration or history, adding dependencies, or changing code, configuration, or HA state: not authorized in this specification phase.

## 9. Proposed future evolutions

- Evaluate reliable provenance from particular TRV integrations to reduce missed actions, subject to verification per device.
- Explore separately presenting the observed physical value and VTherm request (Q-04).
- After measurements, explore an explicit initialization policy before the first order (Q-03); refine operational limits without making an individual latency profile a prerequisite for the baseline.

## 10. Assumptions, open questions, and traceability

**Verified repository facts:** `ThermostatClimate.underlying_changed` drops events within 10 seconds before mode, fan, and setpoint processing, then tracks a setpoint only if `last_sent_temperature` exists and VTherm is active; `UnderlyingClimate._underlying_changed` compares the return with the last sent value using the VTherm step; `UnderlyingClimate.set_temperature` records the clamped value after the service call without device acknowledgement; `ThermostatClimate._send_regulated_temperature` can send a different value per TRV; `BaseThermostat.async_set_temperature` can cancel a preset or trigger an override; `BaseThermostat.update_states` resets the timestamp before controlling heating. `test_underlying_change_follow` currently expects rejection before 10 seconds and adoption after 11 seconds; `test_over_climate_follow_underlying_starts_override_and_echo_ignored` demonstrates the risk of an old return after restoration. User documentation already warns about actions ignored by the timer. Those current expectations will need revisiting during a future implementation, not in this phase.

**Assumption to verify:** rechecking after five seconds of stability of the last candidate value reduces loss of distinct actions without excessive orders; steps, rounding rules, latencies, and provenance vary by device. The `25.6 -> 25.5` case assumes quantization verified for that TRV, not a universal rounding rule.

**Decisions retained and v1.8 changes:** Q-01 preserves the manual setpoint without a preset while OFF without changing priorities. Q-02 retains the sole **sliding** five-second debounce after the last new TRV candidate value (FR-005/FR-008, BR-007/BR-008, AC-003/AC-008), without a ten-second clock. Classification keeps only the last command actually sent per TRV, without a superseded-command history or latency interval: a late `7` after `21` may become a false action and manual preset (FR-003/FR-007, BR-005, AC-006/AC-007). The single cap remains 3 physical attempts caused by internal tracking per VTherm, including retries, deferred sends, induced regulation, and uncertain results; the third is debited and admitted, then tracking suspended, and the fourth rejected before any call (FR-010/BR-008/AC-013). V1.8 replaces v1.7's causal-independence requirement with rearming on every new command received at VTherm's external entry point, including an automation reacting to the TRV and after restart with a lost counter (FR-011/BR-008/AC-012/AC-013); there is no adoption counter or per-TRV cap. Conservative rejection of nearby reports does not prove origin; no-op actions must be coalesced as the cap alone does not bound internal updates.

**Document alignment:** the review, both specifications, and the design retain Q-01, the single interval, the last command per TRV, and the shared cap of 3 between received commands. Commands received at the external entry point rearm without proof of their issuer's original cause; internal tracking calls do not. Q-01 adopts for every admissible explicit TRV temperature action, even while OFF, preset `NONE`, cancellation of old override timers without restoration, and no new timed override; it does not decide the effects of a direct VTherm command. `StateManager.calculate_current_hvac_mode` calculates forced OFF and requested OFF separately; `calculate_current_preset` and `update_current_temp_from_requested` show that current temperature may still depend on preset or window priority, while `BaseThermostat.async_set_temperature` writes the requested temperature and may activate an override or set preset to `NONE`. These are observations about existing behavior, not a claim that the code already tracks actions while OFF. All 14 requirements FR-001 through FR-014, 9 rules BR-001 through BR-009, and 16 scenarios AC-001 through AC-016 remain to be tested, not executed here.

**Q-01 and Q-02 decisions and remaining questions:**

- **Q-01 (decided for admissible TRV actions):** Every admissible explicit TRV temperature action shall update `requested_state.target_temperature`, set `requested_state.preset = NONE`, and cancel any old timed override without restoration or a new timed override, including while OFF. It shall change neither forced effective OFF nor `requested_state.hvac_mode` when it is OFF; window, protection, and OFF priorities remain unchanged. Under forced OFF, the request applies through normal calculation once the cause clears; under manual OFF, after explicit switch-on. This decision does not define the effects of a direct VTherm command on an override.
- **Q-02 (decided):** One cap of 3 physical attempts caused by internal tracking of a device/TRV report per VTherm across all destinations, including retries, deferred sends, induced regulation, and uncertain results; the third is admitted and debited, then tracking suspended, and the fourth rejected before a call. Every new command received by VTherm at its external entry point (UI, automation, or HA service) resets the counter to zero and rearms, even after restart with a lost counter and even when the external sender reacts to the TRV; no proof of independence of its original cause is required. An echo, timer, autonomous regulation, internal callback, tracking-derived order, option toggle, passage of time, or restart alone is not a new received command; deferred consequences and induced regulation retain their internal cause and do not rearm. The only interval is the sliding five-second debounce, without a ten-second clock or superseded-command history. **Accepted, non-blocking risk:** an external HA automation reacting to the TRV can rearm on every turn; the 3 attempts are bounded between received commands, not globally against that loop. Direct control remains possible.

**Questions that can remain future extensions without blocking a safe baseline:**

- **Q-03:** The existing rule of no adoption before the first effective send remains the target behavior; future initialization allowing first adoption requires a separate decision.
